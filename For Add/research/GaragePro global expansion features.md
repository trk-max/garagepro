# Generalise the core before adding verticals

GaragePro already covers most of what a general repair shop needs, so the first thing to build is not a new vertical module. It is a generalised core: a vehicle/asset model that handles motorcycles, trucks, trailers and machines with km, miles or engine hours; extensible line types (fees, sublet, paint materials, storage, travel); configurable workflow statuses; and pluggable tax, invoice, currency and payment adapters. Every one of the 16 target verticals depends on at least one of these, and today they are hardcoded (LABOR/PART only, km only, 14 fixed statuses, a fixed FuelType enum, Tunisia-only tax). In parallel, Tunisia has an urgent compliance and cash agenda. Finance Law 2026 extended TTN El Fatoora e-invoicing to service providers from 1 January 2026. The February 2025 cheque reform cut cleared cheques by **67.9%** and pushed customers to cash and bills of exchange. FTUSA's e-Constat app has digitised insurance claims since November 2025. After that come vertical modules ranked by local relevance and build cost: body/paint with insurer and ADAS records, quick service lanes, fleet and in-house workshops, mobile and towing field service, tyres, EV high-voltage safety, and detailing. Global e-invoicing packs (Saudi ZATCA, UAE, Egypt, Morocco, EU), heavy-truck and agricultural depth, dealership OEM interfaces, auto-glass EDI and inspection-centre integrations come later. They are large, market-specific bets, and the notes show the market leaders win them through partnerships, not in-house builds. Much of the vertical evidence comes from vendor pages and some is unverified background, so each module below should be checked with real shops before it is built.

## Five hardcoded assumptions block every vertical at once

The vertical research has one thread running through it: what makes a tyre centre, truck shop or tractor dealer different usually lives in the data model, not the screens. Heavy-duty software such as Fullbay is built around **multi-unit work orders covering a tractor and its trailers**, DOT inspection workflows and DVIR submission ([SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/products/fullbay-heavy-duty-repair-shop-software); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/industries-software/a/fullbay/)). Fleet software such as Fleetio pulls **engine hours as well as odometer readings from telematics** and works out cost per mile, km *or hour* for each asset ([Fleetio](https://fleetio.com/solutions/fleet-cost-reduction-software)). Lightspeed's dealer system runs one platform across powersports, marine, RV, trailer and outdoor-power-equipment dealers, more than 4,000 of them ([autocorp.ai](https://autocorp.ai/platform/integrations/lightspeed)). GaragePro's `Vehicle` assumes a car with a globally unique plate and a km odometer, so none of this fits. The first build item is therefore an **asset model**. It needs an asset class (car, motorcycle/scooter, light commercial, truck, bus, trailer, agricultural or construction machine, other equipment). It needs several meters per asset (km, miles, engine hours, PTO or reefer hours). It needs identifiers beyond the plate (VIN, frame number, PIN/serial, engine serial, fleet unit number), and parent/child links so a trailer, implement or attachment can sit under a tractor. Plates should be unique per tenant and country, not globally. A plate can be re-issued, and once the planned multi-garage SaaS exists the same car will appear at several garages.

The powertrain enum has the same problem. A single `HYBRID` value cannot tell a mild hybrid from a full hybrid or a plug-in. In 2025, hybrid-electric cars took **34.5%** of EU registrations and plug-ins about **9.4%**, against 17.4% for battery-electric ([ACEA](https://www.acea.auto/pc-registrations/new-car-registrations-1-8-in-2025-battery-electric-17-4-market-share/)). Those categories carry different service intervals and different high-voltage risk. The enum should become a reference table (MHEV, HEV, PHEV, BEV, CNG, LPG, hydrogen, plus a free "other"). It should carry battery capacity, chemistry and charging-port fields, and room for a battery-passport identifier, because EU EV batteries need a QR-linked passport from **18 February 2027** ([Messe Frankfurt](https://automotive.messefrankfurt.com/global/en/news-insights/industry-insights/european-battery-passport.html)).

Line types are the second blocker. `LABOR/PART` cannot hold the economics of most verticals, and the notes show each one charging for something else. Tyre hotels bill **storage fees per season** ([Hotel Anvelope](https://snapcraft.io/hotel-anvelope)). Towing yards bill **daily impound storage** ([GetApp Towbook](https://www.getapp.com/industries-software/a/towbook/)). ADAS calibration is **often sublet** to specialists ([BodyShop Business](https://www.bodyshopbusiness.com/no-matter-if-you-provide-in-house-adas-calibrations-or-sublet-the-bar-for-entry-into-this-field-keeps-changing/)). Car washes run on **recurring memberships** ([DRB Beacon](https://drb.com/getattachment/340a70fe-fcbf-4fca-8ab6-0e45542e140d/beacon_mobile_press_release_-_final.pdf)). GaragePro should add FEE, SUBLET, PAINT_MATERIAL, STORAGE, TRAVEL/CALLOUT, PACKAGE, DISCOUNT and DEPOSIT/CORE. Each line type should carry a tax category, so the same model can later drive US-style taxes where parts and labour are taxed differently. A **part-origin attribute** (new OEM, new aftermarket, used, rebuilt, customer-supplied) belongs at the same layer. California requires invoices to say whether each part is new, used, rebuilt or reconditioned ([BAR Write It Right](https://bar.ca.gov/wir)). In Tunisia, used parts are in high demand while new parts can cost **2–3x more** ([La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/?p=148333)), and a consumer group claims most components on sale are counterfeit ([African Manager](https://africanmanager.com/otic-plus-de-80-des-composantes-automobiles-habits-et-produits-cosmetiques-sont-contrefaits/)). One field serves a compliance rule abroad and a trust problem at home.

The third and fourth blockers are the **14 fixed repair-order statuses** and the single-country tax and identity layer. A collision shop's real states are CCC's production-board stages, from approval through parts, body, paint and reassembly to calibration ([CCC Repair Workflow](https://cccis.com/repair-workflow)). A quick-lube lane needs three states (waiting, in bay, done). A towing job moves through dispatch states. The right design is a **workshop profile**: a vertical template that seeds the status set, checklists, line types and enabled modules, all stored as tenant configuration, which fits the planned organisation/branch SaaS. Tax rates, invoice numbering, stamp duty, ID formats (CIN, tax ID), phone formats, currency precision (millimes vs cents), time zone and contract templates should likewise move behind a per-country "locale pack". Tunisia becomes the first pack, not the only one. The fifth blocker is `PaymentMethod`, covered in the next section because Tunisia needs that change first.

## Tunisia's 2025–2026 regulatory shocks set the first build list

For the home market, three recent events make certain features urgent. **E-invoicing comes first.** Article 53 of Finance Law 2026 (Law 2025-17) extends mandatory El Fatoora e-invoicing in **TEIF XML via Tunisie TradeNet** to service providers from 1 January 2026 ([WMC](https://www.webmanagercenter.com/2026/01/31/560961/facturation-electronique-tunisie-2026-guide-complet/)). The scope is VAT-registered providers on the régime réel ([La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/?p=4306040)), which covers most formal garages. Sources disagree on penalties: either 100–500 TND or a flat 500 TND per paper invoice, capped at 50,000 TND ([efatourti FAQ](https://www.efatourti.tn/faq/)). A postponement amendment passed committee in April 2026 but had not been published in the JORT by October 2026, and one source says sanctions are not yet applied ([efatourti FAQ](https://www.efatourti.tn/faq/)). That uncertainty argues for building now while it is still a differentiator, not for waiting. The module has to generate TEIF, sign it, submit it, and store the TTN reference and QR code on the invoice. The TTN fee is cited at **190 millimes per invoice** ([efatourti](https://www.efatourti.tn/faq/)). Electronic invoices must carry the same mandatory mentions as paper ones ([idaraty.tn](https://idaraty.tn/fr/procedures/utilisation-de-la-facture-electronique)), so the existing VAT and stamp-duty invoice is the right base. Accountants are named as the gatekeepers of this transition ([Business News](https://businessnews.com.tn/2026/01/07/faten-baatout-pointe-les-zones-dombre-et-les-difficultes-dapplication-de-la-facture-electronique/1381654/)). An **accounting export** (journal entries in CSV/Excel, with an Odoo connector as the natural second step given Odoo's Tunisian module ecosystem; [Odoo apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/17.0/payment_konnect)) should ship alongside it. The export doubles as a distribution channel through accountant resellers. Withholding tax (retenue à la source) certificates for corporate clients are likely needed too, but the notes do not confirm the current rules, so verify them with a Tunisian tax adviser before building.

**Cash and credit come second.** Law 41-2024 ended post-dated and guarantee cheques from February 2025. Cleared cheques fell **67.9% in number and 57.9% in value** in the first nine months of 2025, while bills of exchange rose sharply ([Business News](https://businessnews.com.tn/?p=1374473)). A post-reform IACE survey found **cash at 47%** and transfers and lettres de change at 16% each, and found that 72% of cheques had previously been used for deferred payment or guarantees ([AllAfrica/La Presse](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202505290523.html)). Small shops that lost cheque-based instalments report sales halving ([Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2025/03/got-cash-tunisians-grapple-new-restrictions-cheques)). GaragePro already handles partial payments and aged balances. What it lacks is a configurable payment-method table with **LETTRE_DE_CHANGE** (tracking due date, drawee bank and status), mobile wallet and pay-link types. It also lacks **instalment plans** with a schedule and reminders, and a **cash-drawer session** with opening float, counted close and variance. Online payment should arrive as **pay links**, not a checkout overhaul. Konnect has a sandbox SDK with webhooks, aggregates Flouci, cards, wallets and e-DINAR, and holds Tunisia's first Payment Facilitator licence ([Konnect SDK](https://github.com/Konnect-Networks/php-sdk); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2024/12/tunisia-konnect-secures-undisclosed-funding-renew-capital)). That makes Konnect the obvious first gateway, sent over the channel customers already use.

That channel is **WhatsApp**, and it is the third Tunisian priority. Since 1 July 2025 Meta bills per message, and **utility templates inside an open 24-hour customer-service window are free** ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). Garage status updates, quote approvals and invoices are exactly that kind of traffic. International SMS to Tunisia costs roughly **€0.31 per message** ([Messaggio](https://messaggio.com/bulk-sms/tunisia)), which is expensive next to dinar repair tickets, and alphanumeric sender IDs need INT approval ([sent.dm](https://www.sent.dm/resources/tunisia-sms-guide)). Web push already exists. WhatsApp Business should become the main outbound channel, with a local SMS aggregator as fallback.

**The insurance loop** is the fourth item. It is also where body/paint becomes a Tunisian priority rather than a global one. FTUSA's e-Constat app has been recognised by all member insurers since 1 November 2025. It captures photos, geolocation and party details and returns a **PDF with an authenticity QR code** ([L'Economiste Maghrébin](https://www.leconomistemaghrebin.com/2025/10/31/assurances-la-ftusa-fait-entrer-le-constat-amiable-dans-lere-numerique/)). Motor-insurance fraud of about **200 MTND** was detected in 2025 ([AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202510010308.html)), which raises the value of photo evidence. GaragePro should add a claim record on the repair order: insurer, claim and policy numbers, attached e-Constat PDF, assigned expert, an expert-approval status, the excess (franchise) to collect from the customer, and split invoicing between insurer and customer. The existing versioned, line-by-line quote approval maps naturally onto expert agreement. The notes could not document Tunisian insurers' own expert platforms (STAR, GAT, Comar), so this workflow needs validation with two or three body shops.

**Technical-inspection tracking** is the cheapest win. Visite technique is state-run at ATTT centres (automobile.tn lists **26**), with online booking at attt.com.tn ([automobile.tn](https://www.automobile.tn/fr/guide/visite-technique/pourquoi.html); [Tunisie Numérique](https://www.tunisienumerique.com/tunisie-rendez-de-visite-technique-programmes-a-partir-de-cette-date/)). A national parc in which **half the vehicles are over 15 years old** ([Webdo](https://www.webdo.tn/fr/actualite/national/en-tunisie-50-des-voitures-ont-plus-de-15-ans/380532/)) fails inspections often. GaragePro's maintenance forecast and reminder engine needs only an inspection-expiry field, a pre-inspection checklist template and a "convoyage" (drive-to-inspection) service line to turn a legal deadline into booked work. No ATTT API surfaced, so dates are entered by hand.

### Body and paint are the strongest next vertical

Collision is the one vertical where GaragePro's Tunisian strengths (versioned quotes, digital inspection photos, signed handover) line up with a clear local workflow. Elsewhere, collision software centres on the insurer estimate. CCC builds **digital production boards, schedules and checklists** showing where each vehicle sits at every stage ([CCC](https://cccis.com/collision-repairers/shop-management/vehicle-workflow)). It also treats manual **supplement** processing as a main cause of longer cycle time ([CCC DRP](https://utilities.cccis.com/tag/drp/)). GaragePro should add a stage board keyed to the configurable statuses, supplement versions linked to the original claim estimate, paint-and-materials costing, and a cycle-time and touch-time dashboard. It should not build its own estimator. In the Middle East, **Solera Audatex/Qapter** dominates estimating and is now tied to Saudi Arabia's national claims platform Najm ([collisionweek](https://collisionweek.com/?p=37473); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20221006005120/en/Solera-and-Motori-Team-up-to-Bring-New-Artificial-Intelligence-Breakthrough-to-UAE)). Importing those estimates later beats competing with them.

**ADAS calibration records** belong in this module because the liability case is clear and the build is small. Ford/Lincoln and GM now require **pre- and post-repair scans and calibration of every affected ADAS component** ([Autobody News](https://www.autobodynews.com/news/ford-lincoln-and-gm-tighten-adas-repair-standards-in-refreshed-position-statements)). State Farm began demanding calibration reports from glass shops in February 2025 ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/02/25/state-farm-asks-glass-program-shops-to-provide-failed-recalibration-reports-before-oem-glass-approval/)). One analyst puts skipped-calibration lawsuits at **$200,000 to over $1M** ([Autobody News](https://www.autobodynews.com/news/preventing-common-adas-failures-can-save-shops-millions-in-liability-costs-analyst-says)). I-CAR notes that even a one-degree misalignment can make ADAS fail ([I-CAR](https://rts.i-car.com/collision-repair-news/crn-730.html)), so wheel alignment and suspension jobs count as triggers too. The feature set is small: a trigger rule that suggests a calibration line when a job includes windscreen, bumper, mirror, alignment or suspension work; fields for calibration type, equipment, technician and result; required upload of pre-scan, post-scan and calibration PDFs before the invoice can close; and a signed waiver when the customer declines. GaragePro's existing OBD-II reading and quality-control step are natural anchors. The same records serve auto-glass shops and dealer service lanes later.

### Lane, fleet and field modes reuse what GaragePro already has

Three modules extend existing features rather than adding new subsystems. **Lane mode** for quick service builds on the waiting-room queue and kiosk. Oil-change POS systems create the work order at check-in, track bays and technicians, and **print next-service stickers** ([Orderry](https://orderry.com/oil-change-software/)). Practitioners say the profit sits in add-on services, not the oil change: one shop ran 30–40 oil changes a day, with advisors paid partly on upsells ([DealersEdge](https://forums.dealersedge.com/viewtopic.php?p=48688)). So the module needs a plate-lookup express check-in, the existing red/orange/green inspection collapsed into a counter checklist, sticker printing, and tracking of add-on uptake per advisor. One claim that showing OEM intervals at check-in nearly triples filter sales comes from a low-authority source ([Pulse RevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/tk0303)). Treat it as a test hypothesis, not a promise.

**Fleet and in-house workshop mode** builds on the business-client portal. Fleetio treats the **asset, not the customer, as the centre of the record**. It ingests mileage, hours and fuel-card data, handles fault codes and driver inspections with photos, and reports cost per km or hour ([SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/fleetio/); [fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/012103/fleetio)). Fleetio prices from about **$4 per vehicle per month** ([Fleetio pricing](https://fleetio.com/pricing)), which is a useful benchmark for per-asset billing. GaragePro needs an "internal customer" (cost-centre) option so a transport company, municipality or car-rental agency can run its own workshop. It also needs driver defect reports from the portal that become work orders, vehicle-off-road status, and per-asset cost reports. The same mode covers rental fleets, which have their own workshops or partner garages across Tunisia's large organised and informal rental sector. Loaner and courtesy cars, already a known gap, fit here as a small fleet of the garage's own assets with check-out/check-in damage photos taken from the digital inspection component.

**Field mode** serves mobile mechanics, towing, agricultural service and mobile glass. These have similar needs and no dedicated Tunisian software. Towbook's core is digital dispatch acceptance, mobile driver apps with GPS, invoicing to motor clubs and **impound storage management** ([Towbook](https://towbook.com/features); [Azuga](https://www.azuga.com/partners/towbook)). Fullbay gives heavy-duty mobile technicians mobile access and DVIR capture ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/industries-software/a/fullbay/)). GaragePro needs job addresses on a map, travel-time and callout lines, a **van as a stock location** (the existing reservation-based stock module handles this if locations become first-class), on-site signature and pay link, and a tow-intake flow that creates the workshop repair order on arrival. The existing offline hub matters here: field work and Sub-Saharan expansion both need offline capture. Notes on the region stress offline-first tools and mobile money because garages want cash up front ([Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/business/auto/app-bringing-mechanic-services-close-1761866)).

### Tyres, EVs and detailing are smaller modules with clear specs

The **tyre module** should be split in two. Tyre data per wheel position (size, load and speed index, DOT week and year, tread depth, TPMS sensor) is useful everywhere and cheap to add. A full **tyre hotel** is a northern-European product. Vendors such as Compilator offer capacity alerts, barcode and colour labels, seasonal reminders and an API ([Compilator](https://compilator.com/en/tyre-hotel/)). TOPMOTIVE identifies stored sets by registration with QR codes ([TOPMOTIVE](https://topmotive.eu/wp-content/uploads/2Seiter_tmERIK_Reifenhotel_eng.pdf)). Demand is driven by winter-tyre seasons that the notes say barely exist in MENA. Build tread and DOT tracking with the core, and build the hotel when entering Europe. The EU **EPREL** registry holds label data for tyres sold after 25 June 2020 ([SEAI](https://www.seai.ie/business-and-public-sector/energy-tyre-labelling-and-ecodesign/tyre-labelling)), but no public API was confirmed, so plan to source that data from a provider such as WhatTyre ([Tyrepress](https://www.tyrepress.com/tag/eprel/)).

The **EV high-voltage module** is cheap because the HR skills matrix already exists. Germany's DGUV 209-093 (2023) and the UK IMI scheme both define tiered qualifications, from awareness through de-energised work to live HV work ([DGUV](https://publikationen.dguv.de/regelwerk/dguv-informationen/4727/training-for-work-on-vehicles-with-high-voltage-systems); [PMM Online](https://pmmonline.co.uk/article/what-training-do-you-need-to-work-on-evs/)). The UK expects at least IMI Level 2 even without a legal mandate. GaragePro should add qualification scheme, level, issuer and expiry to the skills matrix, and block assignment of HV-tagged jobs to technicians who lack the level. It should add a digital isolation checklist (de-energise, secure against reconnection, verify zero voltage), matching the steps Level 2S training teaches ([EDAG](https://akademie.edag.com/?p=5719)), and a quarantine log for damaged packs following the vfdb guidance on marked outdoor bays ([vfdb](https://www.vfdb.de/media/doc/merkblaetter/MB_06-10_Technical_quarantine.pdf)). The scheme table should be configurable, because no Tunisian equivalent was found. Tunisia sold only **about 600 EVs in 2025** but **about 1,100 by July 2026** after January 2026 tax exemptions ([La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/07/31/coupures-delectricite-bornes-hors-service-longues-files-dattente-le-calvaire-des-proprietaires-de-voitures-electriques/); [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/04/25/voitures-electriques-incitations-renforcees-ventes-en-hausse-et-objectif-de-10-000-bornes-dici-2030/)). This module is therefore a forward-looking differentiator at home and a requirement in Europe, where battery-electric and hybrid cars together took over half of 2025 registrations. Hybrids are the HV work Tunisian garages already see. Because **55% of EVs sold outside China, Europe and the US are Chinese imports** ([All About Industries](https://www.all-about-industries.com/e-car-sales-year-2026-worldwide-30-percent-global-ev-outlook-iea-a-b6ea7c5123829ee8e75aa6c6784e66c2/)), VIN decoding and data sources must cover BYD, Chery, MG and Geely.

**Car wash and detailing** need packages, recurring memberships linked to plates, and coating-warranty records with before/after photos. US tunnel washes add licence-plate recognition and RFID ([DRB](https://drb.com/tunnel/point-of-sale/sitewatch/car_wash_vehicle_identification)). The notes suggest MENA's hand-wash centres need memberships and packages more than tunnel-controller integration. Build the billing primitives first and leave LPR hardware for later. Motorcycle and scooter shops need no separate module once the asset model exists. A quick job card with parts filtered by model fits a high-volume, low-ticket local trade better than a powersports DMS.

### Data integrations decide whether estimates are fast

Every competitor in the notes buys repair and parts data rather than building it, and those integrations are a large part of what keeps their customers. Shopmonkey claims built-in OEM labour times cut estimates **"from half an hour to five minutes"** ([Shopmonkey](https://www.shopmonkey.io/demo-estimates)). Garage Hive bundles Autodata times ([Workshop Software vs Garage Hive](https://workshopsoftware.com/workshop-software-vs-garage-hive/)). **HaynesPro sells all of its data through web services to software partners** ([Infopro Digital Automotive](https://www.infopro-digital-automotive.com/news/improving-technical-capabilities-of-diagnostic-tools-with-haynespro-data/)), and TechMan passes it to garages at no extra cost ([IAAF](https://iaaf.co.uk/news/subscription-free-haynespro-data-access-a-hit-for-technicians-and-reception-staff-using-techman)). That makes HaynesPro the easiest labour-time source for a Peugeot/Renault/VW/Kia-heavy parc. For parts identification, TecDoc is licensed by TecAlliance on negotiated annual terms through partners. Prices and stock come separately ([dev-opla](https://dev-opla.com/resources/what-is-tecdoc-auto-parts-ecommerce-guide)). No Tunisian distributor with an ordering API surfaced, so the local design should be a **supplier-agnostic quote request**: send a part list to several distributors over WhatsApp or PDF and record the replies against the existing purchase-order flow. US entry would instead need PartsTech and Nexpart punch-outs, as Tekmetric and Shopmonkey offer ([Tekmetric](https://support.tekmetric.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041671633); [Shopmonkey](https://support.shopmonkey.io/hc/en-us/articles/38743737653908-Getting-Started-Integrations-with-Shopmonkey)). Unlike the UK, where the government MOT History API gives approved third parties plate-based data ([DfT](https://findtransportdata.dft.gov.uk/dataset/mot-history-api)), no Tunisian or MENA plate-to-vehicle API surfaced in the research, so VIN entry plus manual make/model/engine selection is the practical lookup at home.

AI is the other place GaragePro can stand out. It already has a customer-facing assistant, and competitors' 2026 headline launches are phone receptionists. AutoLeap's AIR (April 2026) answers calls and books work, and one aggregator prices it at **$99 per month** ([BusinessWire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260428092601/en/AutoLeap-Introduces-the-First-AI-Receptionist-for-Auto-Shops-AutoLeap-AIR); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/automotive-shop-software/autoleap/)). Tekmetric launched Phones and AI call scoring ([Shop Owner](https://www.shopownermag.com/tekmetric-unveils-digital-ads-phones-at-tektonic-2026/)). A third-party blog claims Tekmetric still lacks native AI estimate drafting and inspection summaries ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/tekmetric-ai/)). These products target English-speaking markets. A WhatsApp and voice receptionist in Tunisian Arabic and French, plus AI drafting of quotes from complaint text and of customer-friendly inspection summaries, matches how Tunisian customers already contact garages. AI-flagged issues should stay technician-confirmed for liability reasons.

## Global expansion runs through compliance partners, not rewrites

Outside Tunisia, the gating requirement in almost every market is mandatory e-invoicing, and the timetables are near. **Saudi Arabia**'s ZATCA Wave 25 brings in taxpayers above **SAR 187,500** with integration from **1 February 2027** ([ZATCA](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Wave25-E-invoicing.aspx)). The **UAE** goes mandatory for large businesses on 1 January 2027 and for others on 1 July 2027, using Peppol PINT AE through accredited service providers with **51 mandatory fields** ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/02/uae-technical-guidance-mandatory-e-invoicing-fields.html); [Sovos](https://sovos.com/en-gb/vat/tax-rules/uae-e-invoicing/)). **Egypt** runs separate B2B e-invoice and B2C e-receipt systems ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/07/tnf-egypt-new-taxpayers-required-to-comply-with-mandate-to-issue-electronic-receipts-for-b2c-transactions.html)). **Morocco** has the legal basis but its implementing decree was still pending in mid-2026 ([EDICOM](https://edicomgroup.com/fr/blog/maroc-obligation-facturation-electronique)). In Europe, Belgium has required Peppol since 1 January 2026, Poland's KSeF covers all VAT payers from April 2026, French SMEs must issue e-invoices through an approved platform from **1 September 2027**, Germany requires issuance from 2027/2028, and Spain's Verifactu starts in 2027 ([eConnect](https://econnect.eu/en/blog/mandatory-e-invoicing-in-2026-which-countries-are-affected-0); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2026/07/french-e-invoicing-mandate-readiness.html); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/12/tnf-spain-verifactu-invoicing-system-delayed-to-2027.html)). The design lesson is to build El Fatoora as the first implementation of a generic **e-invoice adapter interface** (canonical invoice, then country serialiser, then transmission provider, then stored clearance reference and QR). Then plug in certified access points or approved platforms for each market rather than certifying GaragePro everywhere. The same adapter pattern applies to payments: Paymob covers Egypt, the UAE, KSA and Oman, HyperPay covers KSA and the region ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=4215148); [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21518055/ibrahim-hyperpay-processes-over-25bn-in-monthly-transactions?lang=en)), and embedded BNPL is becoming table stakes in the US, where CCC ONE added Sunbit in April 2026 ([CCC IR](https://ir.cccis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ccc-introduces-consumer-financing-ccc-oner-help-collision-repair/)).

Market order follows from the notes. **Morocco and Algeria** share Tunisia's French/Arabic language mix and brand base. There, French DMS vendors such as Solware and incadea serve dealers and networks ([J2R Auto](https://j2rauto.com/reseaux/eurorepar-car-service-se-developpe-en-algerie-avec-solware-auto); [Solware](https://www.solware.fr/?p=5554)) and leave independents underserved. No Tunisian-specific garage SaaS surfaced in search ([La Fabrique du Net](https://www.lafabriquedunet.fr/logiciels/gestion/facturation/facturation-garage-auto)). The Gulf is worth more money: KSA repair is about **USD 2.3B** per a commercial report ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/ksa-automotive-repair-and-maintenance-market)). But Arabic, ZATCA-ready accounting incumbents such as Qoyod already sell job-card costing and insurance-claim ageing ([Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/fields/accounting-software-for-car-maintenance-centers/)), so GaragePro would compete on workshop depth. France has **69,062 workshops, 83.7% independent** ([Journal de l'Automobile](https://journalauto.com/distribution/les-reseaux-constructeurs-ne-representent-que-163-des-garages-francais/)), but it is a niche play against entrenched EBP and Fiducial and needs the 2027 platform mandate handled. Francophone West Africa fits an offline, mobile-money, WhatsApp-first version of the product.

The deep verticals belong in this later tier because each one sits behind an incumbent standard or integration network that GaragePro would have to join, not out-build. Dealer service runs on DMS platforms whose core includes OEM warranty claims and recall management ([Gartner](https://external.pi.gpi.aws.gartner.com/reviews/market/dealer-management-systems)), and those vendors control third-party access fees ([Car Dealer Magazine](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/car-dealers-fear-huge-bill-increases-as-a-result-of-secret-row-between-suppliers-and-keyloop/282613)). GaragePro's realistic dealer target is the small sub-dealer or agent without a DMS. Its showroom, warranty and claims modules already go part of the way, and recall checks at booking, as LOCO-Soft offers ([Autoservice Praxis](https://www.autoservicepraxis.de/downloadfile/2673554/90007_2020_24_10_I_036-037_n33_Loco_Soft_Villwock_464115_print.pdf)), would be the next addition. Auto glass in the US runs on NAGS pricing and EDI billing to insurer administrators, both weakly sourced in the notes ([beancount.io, low authority](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/03/mobile-auto-glass-windshield-replacement-adas-recalibration-bookkeeping-asc-606-insurance-billing-fmvss-205-agsc-agrss-rolags-section-179-cargo-van-tpa-network-kpi-guide)). Technical-inspection centres use regulator systems: UK MOT equipment reports straight into the DVSA service ([GEA](https://gea.co.uk/learn-about-mot-connected-equipment/)), and Tunisia's are state-run. So GaragePro should consume inspection data (the UK MOT History API, by application; [DfT](https://findtransportdata.dft.gov.uk/dataset/mot-history-api)) rather than build test-lane software. Security-related work is moving behind credentials: SERMI has been mandatory in Italy since February 2025 and was due in the UK from April 2026, alongside per-OEM gateway subscriptions such as Stellantis via AutoAuth ([SicurAUTO](https://www.sicurauto.it/b2b-auto-flotte/sermi-bureau-veritas-dekra-e-kiwa-i-nuovi-cab-italiani/); [CAT Magazine](https://catmag.co.uk/article/sermi-goes-live-in-uk-with-backing-of-22-manufacturers); [Hunter](https://www.hunter.com/adaslink-gateway)). A credential register and an immutable audit log of key-programming and gateway-unlock jobs should ship with EU entry. Heavy-truck VMRS coding and agricultural-dealer integrations are the least supported in the notes (largely unverified background) and should wait for a design-partner customer.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which verticals should GaragePro support?" to "which assumptions stop GaragePro from supporting all of them?" Most target verticals are configurations of the same few primitives: an asset with meters, a job with configurable stages, lines with types and tax categories, a location for stock, and an adapter for invoices and payments. The few that are not, such as dealer DMS, insurer estimating, US glass EDI and inspection lanes, are better joined through integrations than rebuilt. That makes the generalised core the highest-leverage engineering work, and it should be done alongside the planned multi-tenant SaaS migration, because both touch every table. Doing it after a dozen vertical features have been bolted onto car-only, Tunisia-only tables would cost far more.

Competitor evidence points to the strategic edge. US and UK leaders draw steady complaints about price and add-on creep ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/resources/tekmetric-vs-shopmonkey/)), while their moats are data bundles and English-language AI. GaragePro can compete on all-inclusive pricing, the local compliance (El Fatoora, cash and lettre-de-change handling) that foreign products lack, Arabic/French AI on WhatsApp, and distribution through the networks that already organise Tunisian garages. Précisium charges members **550 TND a month**, which shows organised garages will pay for network services ([J2R Auto](https://j2rauto.com/non-classe/precisium-simplante-en-tunisie)). Two risks need early attention: El Fatoora's enforcement date is uncertain, and another "GaragePro" already sells to garages in France, Morocco and francophone Africa ([Payhip](https://payhip.com/b/lvIOB)). Check the trademark before any expansion outside Tunisia.
